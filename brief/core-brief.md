# YouTube Automation — Core Brief

## Current intent

Build a faceless YouTube content business that detects promising trends inside
selected niches and turns the underlying topic/opportunity into original
AI-assisted videos. The operators will not appear on camera.

This is not a clip-reupload or light-remix operation. Trend videos are research
signals; every published video needs an original angle, script, narration,
visual package, edit, and viewer value.

## Business model

- Primary: YouTube Partner Program advertising revenue.
- Later candidates: affiliate revenue, sponsorships, and owned digital products.
- Operating thesis: use automation to shorten research and production time while
  retaining human editorial approval and a recognisable channel perspective.

## Provisional first-release boundary

First-release workflow:

1. collect trend signals from YouTube and external sources;
2. score topics for demand, competition, shelf life, monetisation, and risk;
3. collect multiple reliable sources and create a fact sheet;
4. generate several original angles and select one editorially;
5. draft an original script with source traceability;
6. run factual, copyright, duplication, and policy checks;
7. create licensed/synthetic narration and original or licensed visuals;
8. assemble the video, thumbnail, title, description, and disclosures;
9. require human approval before publishing;
10. collect analytics and feed results back into topic and format scoring.

Automation may prepare drafts and production assets. It may not autonomously
publish in the first release.

## Required decisions

- Channel niche, language, and target audience.
- Existing channel versus a new channel.
- Long-form, Shorts, or both.
- Exact originality, sourcing, and citation standard.
- Voice: human, licensed synthetic voice, or presenter.
- Visual production model and owned/licensed asset sources.
- Publishing cadence and first 90-day success metric.
- Budget and preferred AI/media services.
- YouTube account/API access and production approval owner.

## Safety and platform constraints

- No deceptive impersonation, copyright infringement, fake engagement, or spam.
- No downloading and lightly editing another creator's video.
- No sentence-level rewriting of one source; scripts must synthesize multiple
  sources and add a distinct thesis, explanation, story, or analysis.
- Avoid interchangeable videos produced from a fixed generic template.
- Realistic synthetic or meaningfully altered media must use the applicable
  YouTube AI disclosure.
- Publishing remains human-approved until reliability and policy compliance are demonstrated.
- Credentials and API secrets must not be stored in project documents or source control.
